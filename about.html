A practice built around where technique comes from

The name reflects how the practice approaches technique: traced back to where it was developed and taught, rather than adapted secondhand.

[DR. CRUZ — PORTRAIT]
Founding Surgeon
Dr. Pacifico Cruz, MD
MD, UP College of Medicine·Diplomate, Philippine Board of Plastic Surgery (2024)·Fellowship-trained in Tokyo & Ghent

Dr. Cruz trained in plastic surgery at the University of the Philippines–Philippine General Hospital, one of the country's busiest public referral centers for burns, trauma, and complex reconstruction. That residency shaped an approach to surgery grounded in reconstructive fundamentals before it turned toward aesthetics — the order most plastic surgery training follows, and one Provenance's own case mix still reflects.

After board certification, he pursued two fellowships specifically chosen to train directly under the surgeons who originated the techniques involved, rather than under intermediaries who had learned them secondhand.

Tokyo — Reconstructive Microsurgery (2024–2025)

A full-year fellowship at Nippon Medical School under Dr. Rei Ogawa, spanning reconstructive surgery, microsurgery, keloid management, and lymphedema surgery. Dr. Ogawa's unit is known internationally for its keloid research and its volume of lymphaticovenous anastomosis (LVA) cases — both areas that are otherwise seen infrequently even within plastic surgery training.

The fellowship is the basis for Provenance's Reconstructive & Microsurgery practice, including its keloid management protocol and lymphedema surgery program based at St. Luke's Medical Center, BGC. Following this training, Dr. Cruz performed the first lymphedema surgeries in the Philippines.

Belgium — Aesthetic Plastic Surgery (2025)

A three-month fellowship (March–June 2025) split between Tonnard & Verpaele Plastic Surgery in Ghent and E:MC2 in Sint-Martens-Latem, with additional training in Brussels, under Dr. Patrick Tonnard and Dr. Alexis Verpaele — the surgeons who developed the MACS (Minimal Access Cranial Suspension) facelift and popularized nanofat grafting.

This training underlies Provenance's facelift and fat-grafting techniques, offered here as they were taught rather than as adaptations of them.

Why Provenance

A technique's provenance — who developed it, and who taught it directly — is treated here as part of the information a patient is entitled to, not a footnote. Where a procedure traces to a specific fellowship, that lineage is named on its own page rather than folded into general marketing language.
